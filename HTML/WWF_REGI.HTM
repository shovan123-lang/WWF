SNUKA NO-SHOWS REGINA, SLAUGHTER WALKS OUT WITH A WIN OVER WARRIOR

The WWF's Canadian swing reached the Agridome in Regina on Thursday, July
11, and drew 4,200 paying $63,000 at a $15 average ticket. That's the
smallest crowd and lowest gate of the month, below even Des Moines, and the
third straight night the numbers have dropped since Calgary's $260,000. The
office already cut the ticket to $17 in Saskatoon and went to $15 here, and
it didn't help. The card rated *1/4, the weakest of the four nights in
Alberta and Saskatchewan. Calgary was **, Edmonton and Saskatoon were *1/2
each, and this one was a notch below that. There was some good undercard
wrestling, but the two biggest matches after intermission both came in
under expectations, and one of them went the wrong way three days before
the pay-per-view.

The night started with a no-show. Jimmy Snuka was booked to team with
Bushwhacker Luke against the Beverly Brothers in the opener, since Butch is
still out with his elbow, and Snuka never got to the building. The story
going around the dressing room is that Snuka missed the drive out of
Saskatoon. The crew left the hotel early Thursday for the trip down Highway
11, Snuka wasn't in the lobby, and by the time anyone tracked him down
there was no realistic way to get him to Regina for an 8 PM bell. One
version has him sleeping through his wake-up call. Another has him getting
a late start and turning around after car trouble outside Davidson. Either
way, the office wasn't happy. Snuka is booked in Winnipeg on Friday against
the Berzerker and on WrestleFest Sunday against the Warlord, so they'll be
watching to see if he shows up.

Crush filled in as Luke's partner, which is about as random a team as
you'll see this year. They lost to the Beverlys at 10:12 when Beau pinned
Luke with the flying axhandle after the Coach got involved. It still rated
**1/2, which is a better match than anyone expected out of that pairing.
The Beverlys open WrestleFest against High Energy, and the Coach has now
interfered in both of their house show matches this week.

SATO beat Shane Douglas at 10:43 for ***, the match of the night and a
rerun of their *** opener in Calgary. SATO won with a belly-to-back
superplex this time. After his 42-second DQ loss to Skinner in Saskatoon,
this was the right call. SATO and Douglas have now had two *** matches in
four nights, and the office should keep putting them together.

Big Boss Man beat Skinner at 7:03 for **3/4 with the Boss Man slam. That's
a surprise number for both men. Boss Man's matches with Dino Bravo have
gone 23 minutes for ** and 10 minutes for 1/4*, so the lesson might be that
Boss Man works better in a short match with an opponent who can bump.

The Rockers beat Money Inc. at 19:14 (**3/4) when Shawn Michaels pinned IRS
with a side suplex. That's two straight shows the Rockers have worked where
they were one of the better matches on the card, and they get the Nasty
Boys for the tag titles on Sunday. One thing worth noting: Marty Jannetty's
contract is coming due. He's been as consistent as anyone on this tour, and
with the Rockers in line for the belts, the timing of his deal is something
the office will have to deal with soon. If the plan is to put the titles on
the Rockers in St. Louis, they'll want Jannetty signed before they do it.

Genichiro Tenryu pinned Haku with a powerbomb at 12:09, but it only rated
*3/4. That's the first SWS match on this loop that didn't land. Dino Bravo
beat Koko B. Ware at 8:14 with the side suplex for *.

The second half was rough. Hacksaw Jim Duggan beat Sid Justice by DQ at
10:57 when Harvey Wippleman interfered, and the match rated -**. Sid has
now been in two of the worst matches of the month, -***1/4 with Beefcake in
Edmonton and now this. The ** with Hercules in Saskatoon is the only time
he's cleared one star on the loop. He's being pushed as Hulk Hogan's next
challenger, starting with the angle on Sunday, and the ratings say he needs
to be protected in short matches with strong opponents until then.

The main event was supposed to be the Ultimate Warrior beating Sgt.
Slaughter by DQ, with Gen. Adnan getting involved to keep Slaughter strong
for Hogan. Adnan did interfere against Warrior, but Road Warrior Hawk then
came out and went after Slaughter, and the DQ went the other way. Slaughter
won at 14:08, rated *3/4. Hawk wasn't booked on the show, since LOD were
resting tonight. So Slaughter goes into his WWF Title shot on Sunday with a
win over the Warrior, which is more than the office planned to give him.
There's also now an LOD and Slaughter thread nobody planned. Given
Slaughter's gimmick, the fans will want to see that.

Bottom line: *1/4, the low point of the Canadian swing. SATO and Douglas,
Boss Man and Skinner, and the Rockers held up the undercard, but the Snuka
no-show, the -** Sid match and a main event finish that went the wrong way
made for a rough night. One stop left in Canada, Winnipeg on Friday, then
Milwaukee and St. Louis.
