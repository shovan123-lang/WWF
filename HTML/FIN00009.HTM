THE BARBER SHOP WITH BRUTUS "THE BARBER" BEEFCAKE: GUEST RICKY STEAMBOAT
St. Louis, Busch Stadium | Segment 10 (8:00)

The Barber Shop set is in the ring: two barber chairs, a red-and-white
striped pole, a tall mirror and a stool with a towel, a comb and a can of
shaving cream. The lights come down over the infield.

HOWARD FINKEL: Ladies and gentlemen, please welcome the host of the Barber
Shop... Brutus "The Barber" Beefcake!

Beefcake's music hits and he struts down the long aisle in his tights and a
sleeveless barber smock, snapping a giant pair of hedge shears over his
head. The fans along the rail snip back at him with their fingers. He
climbs in, cuts the air in all four corners and grabs the microphone off
the barber chair.

BRUTUS BEEFCAKE: St. Louis! Welcome to the Barber Shop! Now usually when
somebody sits down in my chair, they're getting a little off the top, a
little off the sides, and if they're real lucky, I take it all. But not
tonight. Tonight the Barber Shop's got a guest who's earned the best seat
in the house. A year ago the doctors told me I was done, that I'd never set
foot in this ring again. So I know a little bit about what this man is
going through. St. Louis, please welcome Ricky "The Dragon" Steamboat!

The Dragon's music hits and the crowd stands. Steamboat comes through the
curtain on crutches, his right knee locked in a heavy brace, wearing his
karate gi top over street clothes. It's a long walk from the stage to the
ring at Busch Stadium, and he takes it slowly, stopping to slap hands along
the rail. Halfway down, the fans start chanting his name. Beefcake meets
him at the ring steps, takes the crutches and holds the middle rope while
Steamboat sits on the apron and swings his good leg through. Beefcake spins
one of the barber chairs around, dusts it off with the towel and helps
Steamboat into it.

BRUTUS BEEFCAKE: Dragon, I've had a lot of guys in that chair, and you're
the first one I'm not going to cut. The people want to hear it from you.
What happened?

Steamboat takes the microphone. The stadium quiets down.

RICKY STEAMBOAT: Thank you, Brutus. And thank you, St. Louis. I had a
speech written for tonight, and I'm going to throw it away, because looking
at all of you standing up for a man on crutches... I didn't expect that.

RICKY STEAMBOAT: July 5th, Des Moines, Iowa. I was in the ring with
Skinner. Three and a half minutes in, he picked me up for a back suplex, I
came down wrong, and I heard my knee go before I felt it. I've been doing
this a long time and I've been hurt before, but I knew. Before the referee
got to me, I knew. The doctors call it a torn anterior cruciate ligament. I
call it the worst sound I've ever heard.

RICKY STEAMBOAT: They tell me it's going to be a long time. Surgery, then
months of rehab. They tell me I might not be the same. And I've heard all
of that before, too. I've been told I was too small. I've been told I was
too nice. I've been told I couldn't beat the Macho Man for the
Intercontinental Title.

The crowd pops. Beefcake points at Steamboat and nods.

RICKY STEAMBOAT: So here's my promise to every one of you. I'm going to do
the work. Every morning, every exercise, every hour of it. And when I come
back, I'm going to come back the same Ricky Steamboat you've always known.
You have my word.

The crowd chants for Steamboat again. Beefcake takes the microphone back.

BRUTUS BEEFCAKE: You hear that, St. Louis? The Dragon is coming back! And
when he does, the Barber's going to be standing right there in his corner,
and anybody who wants to get to Ricky Steamboat is going to have to get
through these first.

Beefcake snaps the shears. He's cut off by music. Skinner walks out onto
the stage, spits on the ramp and grins down toward the ring. He has a
microphone.

SKINNER: Well, ain't this sweet. The Barber Shop's turned into a hospital.
Steamboat, I was there in Des Moines. I heard that knee too. Sounded just
like a gator snapping a dry branch in two. You want to talk about coming
back? Where I come from, when something snaps like that, it don't come
back. You leave it in the swamp. And Beefcake, you wave them scissors at
me, I'll feed 'em to you.

Beefcake throws down the microphone, grabs the shears and heads for the
ropes. The crowd roars for him to go. Before he can step through, Steamboat
grabs the ropes, pulls himself up out of the chair on one leg and catches
Beefcake by the arm. Beefcake turns around. Steamboat shakes his head and
points at his own chest. Beefcake hands him the microphone.

RICKY STEAMBOAT: No, Brutus. Not tonight. He's mine.

Skinner laughs, spits again and backs through the curtain.

RICKY STEAMBOAT: Skinner! You enjoy the next few months. Count every one of
them. Because the first thing I'm going to do when I walk back through that
curtain is come looking for you.

Beefcake hands Steamboat his crutches. Steamboat drops the microphone into
the barber chair and raises a crutch over his head to the crowd. Beefcake
helps him through the ropes and walks him back up the long aisle while the
stadium stands and claps them out. At the top of the ramp, Beefcake turns,
points the shears back at the curtain where Skinner left and snips them
twice.
