Match observations: 

Maple Leaf Gardens
Toronto, Ontario
July 21, 1991
Attendance: 14,200
Average ticket: $18.00
Gate: $255,600
Merchandise: $56,800

PERFECT KEEPS THE GOLD, BUT SATO STEALS THE SHOW

The Legion of Doom were the new champions, Hulk Hogan was in the main
event, and Sid Justice was standing in the Warlord's corner. But the
match Toronto will be talking about this week was the Intercontinental
Title match. SATO took Mr. Perfect to the limit for fourteen minutes,
and had him beaten more than once. When SATO went to the top rope,
Bobby Heenan reached up and grabbed his foot, and Perfect pulled him
down into the Perfect-Plex for the pin at 14:03. Perfect is still the
champion. SATO left to one of the biggest ovations of the night.

The Mountie had already set the bar. Shane Douglas gave him everything
he had, and for eleven minutes the Gardens believed the kid from
Pittsburgh might pull it off. Then the Mountie locked in the Carotid
Control, and Douglas faded and submitted at 11:03. Afterward the
Mountie went for the shock stick, but Jimmy Hart grabbed his arm and
told him to save it.

FIRST DEFENSE FOR THE LEGION

Before the tag title match, Jimmy Hart and the Nasty Boys told Sean
Mooney they were robbed in Philadelphia. Sags carried an empty belt bag
and Knobbs called the Legion's win a loan. Hawk and Animal answered in
the ring. Power and Glory kept them busy for more than eleven minutes,
but Hawk came off the ropes with the flying clothesline on Hercules and
pinned him at 11:40. The Legion of Doom are still the champions.

Animal came through the curtain favoring his right elbow, but he and
Hawk had an answer for the Nasty Boys anyway: Jack Tunney names the
place, and they'll be there. Bring the megaphone, Jimmy.

HOGAN BEATS THE WARLORD, SID WATCHES

Harvey Wippleman told Mooney that Sid Justice would be in the
Warlord's corner tonight to watch and learn. Sid had one line for the
champion: "Look up, Hogan. I'll be there."

Sid did exactly that. He stood in the corner the whole match and never
touched the ropes. Wippleman was not so patient. He got involved when
the Warlord had Hogan in trouble, but Hogan Hulked up, dropped the big
leg and pinned the Warlord at 13:44. When it was over, Sid stepped up
on the apron. Hogan pointed at him, then at the belt. Wippleman pulled
Sid down before anybody threw a punch.

HEENAN CHOOSES

Backstage, Haku came looking for Bobby Heenan before his match with
Jake Roberts. Heenan was standing outside Mr. Perfect's locker room
and told Haku he'd be watching on the monitor. Haku looked at Heenan,
looked at the Intercontinental Title, and walked off alone. Jake
Roberts beat him with the DDT at 8:28, and Heenan never came out.

THE REST OF THE CARD

Shawn Michaels told Mooney that Marty Jannetty had the night off, and
that Owen Hart would find out what one Rocker could do by himself.
"Figure of speech," he added. Michaels and Owen then had one of the
best matches of the night, and Michaels won it with a suplex at 12:04.
Owen offered his hand before the bell. Michaels took his time before
he shook it.

The Undertaker beat Koko B. Ware with the Tombstone at 6:03 after a
better fight than anybody expected from Koko.

Hacksaw Jim Duggan beat Sgt. Slaughter by disqualification at 12:41
when General Adnan came in with the flag pole. Duggan cleared them
both out with the 2x4.

Dino Bravo, wrestling in Canada for the first time since Jimmy Hart
fired him, told the Toronto fans "ce soir, c'est pour vous," and then
pinned Skinner with the side suplex at 7:06. The crowd was with him
all the way.

The Bushwhackers opened the show. Butch is back from his elbow injury,
and he and Luke celebrated by licking Sean Mooney, then beat the Orient
Express when Luke pinned Tanaka with the battering ram at 8:29.
