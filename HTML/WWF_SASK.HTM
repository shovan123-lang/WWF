HOGAN KEEPS HIS BELT, BUT SID JUSTICE IS WATCHING

SASKATOON - Hulk Hogan came to Saskatchewan Place on Wednesday night in
front of 6,500 fans, and he left with his WWF championship belt still
around his waist. But the man everybody was watching at the end of the
night wasn't Hogan, and it wasn't the Warlord. It was the towering figure
standing at ringside with his arms folded.

Hogan pinned the Warlord with his trademark leg drop after 12 minutes of a
power struggle in the main event. Harvey Wippleman, the Warlord's pint-
sized manager, did everything he could to swing it, climbing on the apron
and getting in the Hulkster's way. Sid Justice, Wippleman's other client
and the man who saved Hogan from a beating at last weekend's television
taping in Vancouver, stood at ringside for the whole match and never lifted
a finger. When Hogan posed for the crowd afterward, Sid was still standing
there, staring at the belt. Hogan defends that title against Sgt. Slaughter
on Sunday at Busch Stadium in St. Louis, and he might want to keep one eye
over his shoulder.

The semi-main event was for the World Tag Team Championship, and the Nasty
Boys left town with the belts. They didn't earn them. Brian Knobbs and
Jerry Sags spent most of 16 minutes getting pounded by the Legion of Doom.
Then Sags got Hawk tangled up on the floor, slid back in the ring, and took
the count-out win. Paul Ellering was in the middle of it at ringside, and
in the confusion Hawk never made it back in. The fans wanted blood, and
Hawk and Animal gave it to them. As the Nasty Boys tried to sneak up the
aisle, LOD dragged Knobbs back into the ring and finished him off with the
Doomsday Device, Animal holding him up on his shoulders and Hawk flying off
the top rope with a clothesline. The champions keep the belts, but nobody
in the building thought they deserved them.

The best wrestling of the night came before intermission. Tito Santana
pinned Rick Martel in a 16-minute battle that had the crowd up for every
near fall, finishing with his flying forearm. Santana faces the Mountie on
Sunday, and on this showing he's ready. Owen Hart and Koko B. Ware, the
high-flying team known as High Energy, beat the Orient Express in 15
minutes when Koko hit Tanaka with his Ghostbuster. They meet the Beverly
Brothers in St. Louis.

The local crowd's favourite upset came from Shane Douglas, who rolled up
the hulking Berzerker with a small package even after Mr. Fuji tried to get
involved. Big Boss Man beat Dino Bravo with the Boss Man Slam in spite of
interference from Jimmy Hart.

After intermission, Sid Justice made quick work of Hercules with his
powerbomb, with Wippleman at ringside again. The Undertaker, with Paul
Bearer and his urn, put away Hacksaw Jim Duggan with the Tombstone in nine
minutes. Duggan is Hogan's hand-picked special enforcer for Sunday's main
event.

The night started strangely. Skinner, the swamp-dwelling alligator hunter,
won the opening match by disqualification in under a minute. The Japanese
star SATO got himself thrown out before most of the crowd had found their
seats.

The WWF's Canadian swing continues Thursday night in Regina.
