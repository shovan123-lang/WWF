WWF SUPERSTARS REVIEW
Aired Saturday, July 13, 1991 | Taped July 7, BC Place, Vancouver

SUPERSTARS GO-HOME: SIX SQUASHES, A NEW IC CHALLENGER AND A LOT OF PATCHWORK

This was the go-home show for WrestleFest, and anyone who has followed the
last week of WWF house shows could see the tape and glue holding it
together. The matches were taped July 7 in Vancouver, two days before
Edmonton took out Earthquake and Greg Valentine. By the time it aired, the
office had cut one taped segment, recorded about a dozen inserts during the
Canadian swing, and changed two matches on the pay-per-view. Considering
all of that, it came off better than it had any right to. It was a
well-produced hour that told you what you needed to know for Sunday, even
if there wasn't a match on it worth going out of your way for.

The six matches were all squashes, as you'd expect. The Rockers, Sid
Justice, the Beverly Brothers, the Ultimate Warrior, Big Boss Man and Hulk
Hogan all beat enhancement guys in under four minutes. The Rockers looked
the sharpest. Sid looked like a monster for two and a half minutes, which
is exactly how they should be using him, given that his house show matches
have gone -***1/4 with Beefcake, ** with Hercules and -** with Duggan this
month.

The big news was the Intercontinental title. In the taped segment from
Vancouver, Valentine challenged Mr. Perfect and Perfect accepted. That was
cut, and in its place Sean Mooney announced that Valentine is out for
several weeks and that Jack Tunney had named Owen Hart as the new
challenger. Owen cut a fired-up promo bringing up the Edmonton match, where
Heenan grabbed his boot, and Perfect and Heenan answered with a good bit
where Gene asked about Perfect walking out on the Barbarian in Winnipeg.
Perfect's line about not carrying anybody was the best thing on the show.
It's also the first time the Barbarian thing has been acknowledged on TV,
so they're at least keeping the option open.

This is the right call. Owen has been the best worker on the tour, with
***1/2 against Martel in Calgary and ***1/2 in the Winnipeg opener. The
concern is whether they're willing to do anything with him. Perfect vs.
Tito is already being set up for SummerSlam on the episodes taped in
Vancouver, so the safe bet is that Owen is getting a strong showing and not
the belt. The opener changes to Koko B. Ware and Bushwhacker Luke against
the Beverlys, with Butch still out, and Koko and Luke got a short insert
that was fine for what it was.

The "WWF on the Road" segment was an interesting touch. With no cameras at
the house shows, Mooney ran down the Canadian results over still photos:
Slaughter's DQ win over the Warrior in Regina when Road Warrior Hawk
attacked him, Boss Man pinning the Mountie in Winnipeg, and IRS beating
Virgil with the Penalty. The WWF rarely acknowledges house show results on
TV, and it made the house show angles feel like they mattered. LOD followed
with a promo telling Slaughter he's next after Power & Glory. Nobody
planned an LOD and Slaughter program, but between Hawk's run-in in Regina
and this promo, they've got one if they want it.

The Mountie and Slaughter promos were both re-recorded after the tour. The
Mountie was furious about losing to Boss Man, and Slaughter bragged about
beating the Warrior. Slaughter needed it. Going into a title match with
Hogan, a DQ win over the Warrior is about the only thing he has to point
to.

The Hogan segment closed the go-home build. Slaughter and Adnan jumped
Hogan after his squash, Sid made the save and then wouldn't let Hogan raise
his hand, and Hogan named Hacksaw Jim Duggan as his enforcer instead of
Sid. The last shot was Sid watching from the side. That's the angle for
Sunday, and it was laid out clearly.

The undercard got the usual pre-PPV package: Tenryu and Kitao got a profile
that was better produced than most WWF features, Demolition answered them,
the Funeral Parlor was the Funeral Parlor, Typhoon replacing Earthquake
against Jake got a promo, Jake was creepy, and Steamboat got an injury
update with word that he'll speak at WrestleFest.

A couple of things you didn't see. Jimmy Snuka, who no-showed Regina,
appeared only in a Wippleman line about the Warlord. Davey Boy Smith, who
is finishing an All Japan tour and has caused a lot of grumbling in the
office over it, wasn't mentioned at all. A preview tease of a Jimmy Hart
"big announcement," which was supposed to be the Natural Disasters
reuniting, was dropped, since Earthquake's neck injury will likely keep him
out through SummerSlam. The next episode was taped with that reunion angle
on it, so expect more surgery on next week's show.

Bottom line: no match worth seeing, but an effective go-home show. It
explained every change, built Owen into a credible challenger in about
three minutes, and made the main event angle easy to follow. Considering
they had to rebuild a third of the show after the tape was already in the
can, the production team earned their money this week.
