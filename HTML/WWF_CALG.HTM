OWEN STEALS THE SHOW AT HOME AS ANGLES BUILD TOWARD ST. LOUIS

WWF ran the Olympic Saddledome in Calgary on Monday, July 8, the night
after the Superstars tapings in Vancouver, and drew 13,000 paying $260,000
at a $20 average ticket. That's the second-biggest gate of the month behind
MSG's $543,200, and well ahead of Anaheim ($105,580) and Des Moines
($90,000). Nobody had to sell this crowd on Owen Hart. The card came in at
**, which makes it the best-rated show of the July loop so far, ahead of
MSG (*1/2), Anaheim (1/2*) and Des Moines (1/4*). It still had two long
matches near the top that dragged it down.

The big story backstage was who wasn't there. Calgary is Davey Boy Smith's
home market, and the British Bulldog was not on the card because he's
finishing out a full All Japan tour that runs through the Budokan show on
July 27. That has not gone over well with the WWF office. He also misses
WrestleFest in St. Louis on Sunday, and Greg Valentine was moved into his
Intercontinental Title shot with Mr. Perfect. Nobody in the company is
talking about it publicly, and the absence wasn't acknowledged to the
crowd. But you'd be hard pressed to find a worse market for the office to
be without him.

Owen Hart vs. Rick Martel was the match of the night at ***1/2. Owen won
with the missile dropkick at 18:10, and the building was with him for every
near fall. Martel hasn't been used much lately, and this was a reminder
that he can still go with anybody. Owen is working the WrestleFest opener
with Koko B. Ware against the Beverly Brothers, but on this kind of night
he looks like a singles act.

The opener was a surprise. SATO, one of the SWS wrestlers working the loop
as part of the WWF and SWS relationship, pinned Shane Douglas with a
layback suplex at 10:21, and it rated ***. Douglas got plenty of offense
in. This is the second time in a week the report has said he deserves more
opportunities.

Legion of Doom beat Money Inc. at 17:09 (***). Animal pinned IRS with a
scoop powerslam after Virgil came down and cut IRS off at ringside, picking
up from the angle in Anaheim. Jimmy Hart also got involved against Hawk.
LOD have Power & Glory at WrestleFest, and Money Inc. are clearly being
pointed at the tag titles. Virgil continues to be a thorn in DiBiase's
side, and he gets DiBiase one on one on Sunday.

Big Boss Man beat Earthquake by DQ at 8:08 (3/4*) when Jimmy Hart got
involved. After the bell the Mountie ran in and handcuffed Boss Man to the
ropes. That is the angle for Boss Man vs. the Mountie, and they're laying
it in before Boss Man goes to ringside for Mountie vs. Tito Santana at
WrestleFest. Expect a Jailhouse Match before long.

The Rockers beat the Orient Express at 15:28 (**1/4) when Jannetty pinned
Kato with the Showstopper. It was fine, but these two teams should do
better than this. The Rockers get the Nasty Boys for the tag titles on
Sunday.

The Undertaker pinned Jimmy Snuka with the Tombstone in 7:34 (*1/4).
Keeping it short was the right call given how Taker's matches with Warrior
have rated this month. The Ultimate Warrior was not on the card.

The two long matches at the top are where the show sagged. Mr. Perfect
retained the IC Title over Tito Santana in 24:16, rated **1/4. Bobby Heenan
interfered against Tito to set up the pin. That is way too long for what it
was. Two nights earlier in Anaheim, Tito and the Mountie had a **** match
in under 20 minutes. Perfect vs. Tito looks like the direction for the IC
Title later this summer, so this is a pairing worth getting right.

The main event was Hulk Hogan and Jim Duggan over Sgt. Slaughter and
Warlord at 21:52, rated 3/4*. Hogan pinned Warlord with the legdrop. Harvey
Wippleman got involved against Hogan, then stood on the floor jawing at him
after the match. That matters because Wippleman's other client is Sid
Justice, who made the save for Hogan at the Superstars tapings the night
before. Hogan picked Duggan as his WrestleFest enforcer instead of Sid.
Warlord matches over 20 minutes keep rating poorly; his 24:49 with Virgil
in Des Moines went -*1/2. He shouldn't be in matches that long.

Bottom line: the strongest card of the loop, carried by Owen, SATO and LOD.
The two biggest matches both ran 20-plus minutes and fell flat. The angles
that matter going into St. Louis all got pushed forward: Mountie and Boss
Man, Virgil vs. Money Inc., and Wippleman picking at Hogan.
