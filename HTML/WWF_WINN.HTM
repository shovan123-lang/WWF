WINNIPEG SAVES THE SWING: BEST CARD OF THE MONTH AND A $202,000 GATE

The WWF closed its Canadian swing at the Winnipeg Arena on Friday, July 12,
and it was the best night of the month in the ring and one of the best at
the box office. They drew 10,100 paying $202,000 at a $20 average ticket.
That's the third-biggest gate of July behind MSG and Calgary, edging out
Edmonton by $1,000, and it's a big recovery from Regina's $63,000 the night
before. The ticket went back up to $20 and the crowd still came, which
suggests the problem in Saskatchewan was the markets and not the price. The
card rated **1/4, the best of the tour so far, a quarter star ahead of
Calgary. July is now at $1,575,280 with Milwaukee and the pay-per-view
still to come.

The opener was the match of the night. High Energy beat Power & Glory at
16:29 for ***1/2 when Owen Hart pinned Paul Roma with the Rocket Launcher.
Owen has now had a match rated **3/4 or better on all four of his shows
this week: ***1/2 with Martel in Calgary, **3/4 with Perfect in Edmonton,
*** with Koko against the Orient Express in Saskatoon, and now this. He
goes into WrestleFest in the opener against the Beverly Brothers, and
there's a decent argument he's being wasted there.

The second-best match was Tito Santana and Jim Duggan over Mr. Perfect and
the Barbarian at 14:22 for ***. Tito pinned the Barbarian with the flying
forearm after Bobby Heenan got involved against Duggan. The finish came with a 
twist. With the Barbarian getting worked over by
Tito and Duggan, he reached for the tag, and Mr. Perfect hopped off the
apron and walked up the aisle with Bobby Heenan, leaving his partner alone
in the ring. Tito hit the flying forearm a few seconds later. Heenan was
shouting the whole way to the back that Perfect had an Intercontinental
Title to defend on Sunday and wasn't going to get hurt carrying the
Barbarian. It got a big reaction, and it's an interesting wrinkle with both
men in the Heenan Family. Whether it goes anywhere is another question, but
the Barbarian didn't look happy getting off the mat. Tito has now had
***1/4 with Martel in Saskatoon and *** here, and he faces the Mountie on
Sunday. That's Duggan's best match of the loop, after a 9-minute loss to
the Undertaker in Saskatoon and a -** match with Sid in Regina.

The main event was Hulk Hogan and Big Boss Man over Sgt. Slaughter and the
Mountie at 16:33, rated **3/4. Boss Man pinned the Mountie with the Boss
Man slam, getting a little payback for being handcuffed to the ropes in
Calgary, and Jimmy Hart's interference didn't stop it. That's the best
rating Hogan has had in July by a mile, after 3/4* in the Calgary tag and a
DUD with the Warlord in Saskatoon. Slaughter never took a fall, which was
the point, and after his DQ win over the Warrior in Regina he goes into St.
Louis looking stronger than he has all month. Boss Man has now had **3/4 on
back-to-back nights.

Irwin R. Schyster made Virgil pass out in the Penalty at 12:06 (**1/4) with
Jimmy Hart's help. That's IRS's first win over Virgil after being cut off
in Calgary and pinned in Edmonton, and it's a setup for Virgil against Ted
DiBiase on Sunday. The Undertaker beat Hercules with the Tombstone at 8:04
for **1/2. Hercules worked twice on the card, which is not ideal, but he
held up his end both times. Jake Roberts pinned Rick Martel with the DDT at
12:18 for **. Martel has lost to Owen, Tito and Jake in five days, and he's
been a reliable hand in all three.

Jimmy Snuka showed up. After no-showing Regina, he was in the building for
his match with the Berzerker and won with the Superfly splash at 8:01, even
with Mr. Fuji interfering. It only rated 3/4*, but the office mostly wanted
him there, and he's on the pay-per-view against the Warlord. Typhoon
squashed Shane Douglas with the splash at 6:59 (*), which was the idea,
since he replaces his injured partner Earthquake against Jake on Sunday.

The low point was Koji Kitao over Crush with a big boot at 9:04, rated
-1/4*. Crush has now been in two of the worst matches of the month, after
the -***1/4 with Beefcake in Anaheim, and the Kitao singles experiment
didn't work. Kitao is much better in a tag with Tenryu, which is how he'll
be used on Sunday against Demolition.

Bottom line: **1/4 and $202,000, the best card of the tour and a strong
finish to Canada. Every planned finish went as booked. The two worries
going into the weekend are the IC title match, where Greg Valentine's neck
still needs a replacement challenger, and Sid Justice, who has to look like
a monster on Sunday after a month of bad ratings. The tour heads to
Milwaukee on Saturday, then Busch Stadium.
