Match observations: 

Hara Arena
Dayton, Ohio
July 19, 1991
Attendance: 4,000
Average ticket: $12.00
Gate: $48,000
Merch: $10,000

The WWF rolled out of Richfield and into Dayton's Hara Arena on Friday
night. No Hulk Hogan on this one, but the fans got a main event that
went nearly eighteen minutes and a few surprises along the way.

Jimmy "Superfly" Snuka opened the show by flying off the top rope onto
Skinner with the Superfly Splash at 6:11. Skinner spent most of the
match spitting tobacco juice in Snuka's direction, and the crowd loved
seeing the gator hunter flattened.

High Energy and the Orient Express went nearly eleven minutes in a fast
paced tag match. Mr. Fuji tried to swing it when he cracked Koko B.
Ware with his cane behind the referee's back, but Owen Hart stayed in
the fight and pinned Kato at 10:58 to send Fuji's team home with a loss.

The match of the night came next, and it came from an unlikely place.
Rick Martel and SATO went ten minutes at a pace the Hara Arena hasn't
seen in a long time, trading near falls until the crowd was on its
feet. Then the Model did what the Model does. Martel rolled SATO up,
put his feet on the ropes for leverage, and the referee never saw it.
1-2-3 at 10:06. SATO argued with the official all the way to the back.
Martel just fixed his hair.

Dino Bravo's first night without Jimmy Hart was not a pretty one. Hart
brought Typhoon to the ring looking for revenge for the press slam in
Richfield, and the two big men spent seven minutes leaning on each
other in a match the crowd never got into. But when it mattered, Bravo
knocked Hart off the ring apron, lifted all 400-plus pounds of Typhoon
and dropped him with a side suplex for the pin at 7:04. The match was
ugly, but the suplex woke up the building, and Bravo left to a real
cheer.

Virgil's night went the other way. I.R.S. came after the Million Dollar
Belt again, attempted to hit Virgil with his steel briefcase. Rowdy Roddy 
Piper came charging down the aisle to stop him. Piper got to I.R.S., 
all right, right in front of the referee, who had no choice but to disqualify 
Virgil at 8:06. I.R.S. gets the win, but Virgil walked out with the belt over 
his shoulder, and Piper and I.R.S. had to be pulled apart.

Shawn Michaels worked alone tonight against Paul Roma, and the Rocker
didn't seem to miss his partner one bit. Michaels finished Roma with a
suplex at 10:55 and celebrated in the ring by himself. Whether that
should worry Marty Jannetty is anyone's guess.

The Undertaker put Hacksaw Jim Duggan away with the Tombstone at 8:06.
Duggan got his shots in with the two-by-four and had the Dead Man
staggering, but Paul Bearer raised the urn, Duggan turned to deal with
him, and the Undertaker was waiting.

The main event saw the Big Boss Man and Sid Justice fight for nearly
eighteen minutes. Boss Man took everything Sid had and kept coming
back, and when Sid finally started to fade, Harvey Wippleman jumped up
and grabbed Boss Man's leg. The referee called for the bell at 17:51
and gave the match to Boss Man by disqualification. Sid wasn't
finished. He planted Boss Man with a powerbomb after the bell and stood
over him, holding an imaginary title belt over his head. Hulk Hogan's
name never came up, but everyone in Dayton knew who that message was
for.
