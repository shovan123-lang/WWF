Match observations

PRIME TIME WRESTLINGAired: July 22, 1991 (USA Network)
Matches taped: Richfield Coliseum, Richfield, Ohio, July 18, 1991, and Madison Square Garden, New York, July 1, 1991

Bobby Heenan and Sean Mooney opened two hours of Prime Time with the news from Philadelphia: the Legion of Doom are the new Tag Team Champions. Heenan put the blame on Jimmy Hart's megaphone, and he wasn't done with Jimmy Hart.

Tito Santana pinned Dino Bravo with the flying forearm at 10:45 after Bravo turned his back on the megaphone Hart held out to him. Hart fired Bravo on the spot, and Bravo press slammed him over the top rope to the floor. "Best thing anybody's done with Jimmy Hart in years," said Heenan, who then had to explain tripping Bravo in Philadelphia. He was tying his shoe. Mooney pointed out that he wears loafers.

Mooney's Spectrum Report showed the end of the 56-minute Death Match, Hawk taking all seven falls, and Sid Justice walking out on Sgt. Slaughter without tagging in. Then Hawk and Animal walked into the studio with the belts. Animal promised the Nasty Boys their rematch, and Heenan moved his chair.

The Rockers and Demolition fought to a double countout at 16:06 when all four men brawled on the floor, and Shawn Michaels left Marty Jannetty standing alone in the ring. Heenan made Mooney roll the replay twice and reminded everyone that Michaels celebrated by himself in Philadelphia. Mooney's answer: they won.

The second hour went back to the Garden for the Warlord's win over Greg Valentine on July 1, with Harvey Wippleman in his corner. The Hammer had the figure-four locked in before the Full Nelson ended it, eight days before the neck injury that has him out until late August. Back in Richfield, Hercules ran into Jake Roberts' DDT at 8:04. Mooney noted Jake's nod to Paul Bearer in Philadelphia and declined to guess what it meant.

Heenan swore the Heenan family has never been stronger. Then the Legion of Doom hit the Doomsday Device on Haku at 12:03 while the Barbarian took his time coming off the apron. Heenan called it strategy, and ordered both men to his office.
