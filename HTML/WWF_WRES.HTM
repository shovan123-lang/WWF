Match observations: 

WRESTLEFEST '91: SID SENDS A MESSAGE
St. Louis, Mo. | Busch Stadium | July 14, 1991

Hulk Hogan is still the WWF Champion. Sgt. Slaughter is finished. And by
the time the fireworks went up over Busch Stadium, three men had made sure
nobody in St. Louis would remember either of those things first.

With Hacksaw Jim Duggan wearing the referee's stripes, Hogan and Slaughter
fought for nearly twenty minutes. General Adnan tried to get involved
twice, and twice Duggan ran him off the apron. Slaughter had the camel
clutch locked in and Hogan's arm sagging, but the Hulkster powered out,
made his comeback, and dropped the leg at 19:38. Duggan's three-count was
the loudest of the night.

It didn't last. Sid Justice came over the barricade with Harvey Wippleman
behind him, clubbed Duggan from behind and lifted Hogan into the powerbomb.
He planted the champion in the middle of the ring, then did the same to
Duggan. Sid was standing over Hogan with the belt in his hands when the
Legion of Doom hit the ring. Hawk and Animal cleared out Sid and Wippleman,
and Sid backed up the aisle with his eyes on the gold the whole way.

Earlier, the Ultimate Warrior's war with the Undertaker took its darkest
turn yet. Paul Bearer's urn caught Warrior behind the referee's back, and
the Tombstone did the rest at 12:08. Warrior didn't get up. The Undertaker
and Bearer rolled out the casket and had Warrior halfway in when Jake
Roberts finally came down the aisle with Damien. Jake took his time getting
there. Warrior was helped to the back holding his neck, and WWF officials
say he will be out of action indefinitely.

The tag team title match was supposed to be the night the Rockers finally
took the belts. Instead, Jerry Sags came off the top with an elbow on Marty
Jannetty at 16:50, and the Nasty Boys walked out still champions. Shawn
Michaels didn't take it well. He was in Jannetty's face before the Nastys
had reached the aisle, the two of them started shoving, and it took a few
long seconds before they backed away from each other. They walked to the
back several steps apart. The Nastys, meanwhile, will want to keep an eye
on the Legion of Doom.

Tito Santana stole the show. With Big Boss Man at ringside to keep Jimmy
Hart honest, Tito and the Mountie went fifteen minutes before Tito landed
the flying forearm for the pin. It was the biggest win of Tito's year, and
it puts him squarely in line for another shot at the Intercontinental
Title. The Mountie got his payback after the bell, jamming his shock stick
into Boss Man's ribs and leaving him on the floor.

Mr. Perfect is still Intercontinental Champion, but Owen Hart made him earn
it. Owen hit everything, including the Rocket Launcher, and had Perfect
beaten at least twice before Bobby Heenan dragged the referee out of the
ring. Perfect rolled Owen up while Owen was arguing with Heenan and held on
for the three at 15:14.

Ted DiBiase beat Virgil with a back suplex after Jimmy Hart got involved,
but Virgil had the last word. When DiBiase turned his back, Virgil grabbed
the Million Dollar Belt and ran for the aisle with it. DiBiase and IRS cut
him off at the top of the ramp and were putting the boots to him when
"Rowdy" Roddy Piper came through the curtain and sent them both scrambling.
Piper and DiBiase stood nose to nose for a long moment before DiBiase
backed off. Virgil left Busch Stadium with the belt.
